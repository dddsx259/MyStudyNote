1. Hypothesis
Cantor turned infinity from a vague intuition into a comparable mathematical object (cardinality). This single abstraction move is simultaneously beauty (diagonal argument), utility (ZFC as foundation language), and a "Way" leap (redefining what math can speak of) — three faces of one act, not three separate achievements.

2. Arguments
- Way (before/after): Aristotle potential infinity / Gauss rejected completed infinity → Cantor made infinity countable/comparable/arithmetic.
- Beauty (derived): diagonal argument = one-line "list-and-flip"; aesthetic value rests on the Way leap it performs.
- Utility (derived): ZFC became 20th-c math foundation; spilled into CS/logic.
- Unity: same object (cardinality of infinity) is aesthetic object + foundation tech + expansion of math's speakable domain.
