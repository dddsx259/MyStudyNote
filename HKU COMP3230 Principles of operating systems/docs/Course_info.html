Course Description and Aims

Objective To present the fundamentals of operating systems, to study the detailed operation and principles behind the design of various components of an operating system.

Topics include: operating system structures, process and thread, CPU scheduling, process synchronization, deadlocks, memory management, file systems, I/O systems and device driver, mass-storage structure and disk scheduling.

On successful completion of the course, students should be able to:
ILO 1 -[Fundamentals] discuss the characteristics of different structures of the Operating Systems (such as microkernel, layered, virtualization, etc.) and identify the core functions of the Operating Systems.
ILO 2 -[Principles] explain the principles behind the core functions and compare the algorithms on which the core functions of the Operating Systems are built on.

ILO 2a - explain how OS manages processes/ threads and discuss the mechanisms and policies in efficiently sharing of CPU resources.
ILO 2b - describe the principles and techniques used by OS in effectively virtualizing memory resources.
ILO 2c - explain the underlying causes of concurrency and deadlock issues and describe the principles and techniques used by OS to support concurrency and synchronization control.
ILO 2d - describe the principles and techniques used by OS to support persistent data storage.
ILO 3 -[Performance] analyze and evaluate the algorithms of the core functions of the Operating Systems and explain the major performance issues with regard to the core functions.
ILO 4 -[Practicability] demonstrate knowledge in applying system software and tools available in modern operating system (such as threads, system calls, semaphores, etc.) for software development.

General Course Grade Descriptors
Course Grade Description
A Exceptionally good performance demonstrating a superior understanding of the subject matter over a wide range of topics, a skillful use of concepts and/or materials even in unfamiliar contexts, and ability to analyze and evaluate problems.
B Good performance demonstrating capacity to use the appropriate concepts, a good understanding of much of the subject matter over a wide range of topics, and an ability to handle the problems and materials encountered in the course.
C Adequate performance demonstrating an adequate understanding of the basic subject matter and an ability to handle relatively simple problems.
D Minimally acceptable performance demonstrating at least partial familiarity with the subject matter and some capacity to deal with relatively simple problems, but also showing misunderstanding of the material and significant inaccuracies or errors.
F Unacceptable performance demonstrating unfamiliarity with the subject matter, and lack of capacity to deal with relatively simple problems, and also demonstrating deficiencies serious enough to make it advisable to retake the course.
